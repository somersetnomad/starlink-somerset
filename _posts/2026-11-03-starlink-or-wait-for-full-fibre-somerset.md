<div class="answer" markdown="1">
**Short answer:** wait for full fibre only if your **exact address** is in a confirmed build with a rough date. Some rural homes are "deferred", "under review" or in no plan at all, and the gigabit voucher scheme is closed to new projects here. If that's you, waiting could mean years. Starlink home plans were on **rolling monthly terms** when last checked, so you can use it now and cancel when fibre turns up.
</div>

"Fibre is supposed to be coming, so should I just hang on?" The honest answer depends on one thing that's easy to skip: what the government's own data says about **your property**, not your parish or your postcode.[^1]

## Quick decision

<div class="table-scroll" markdown="1">

| What the data says about your address | What I'd do |
|---|---|
| Full fibre already available | Get fibre. Starlink isn't the answer here. |
| In a Project Gigabit contract, "Initial" scope, and the builder has given you a date | Wait, or use what you have until it's live |
| In a contract but "Deferred" scope | Don't count on it. Consider Starlink as a bridge. |
| "Under Review" | Uncertain. A supplier says it plans to build, but it isn't verified. |
| "White" and not in any contract | No gigabit build expected within three years. Starlink, or the USO if you're under 10Mbps. |

</div>

The categories come from BDUK, the government body running Project Gigabit. The next section shows how to look yours up.

## How to check your own address

A postcode can cover several properties with very different connections, so a postcode checker only gets you so far. BDUK's data works at the level of individual properties instead.

### Step 1: find your UPRN

Every address in Great Britain has a Unique Property Reference Number (UPRN). You can look yours up free on [FindMyAddress](https://www.findmyaddress.co.uk/), which is run by GeoPlace for councils and Ordnance Survey.[^2]

### Step 2: check BDUK's premises data

BDUK publishes [premises-level data on GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/collections/open-market-review-omr-determinations-and-bduk-plans) showing which properties already have gigabit broadband, which have commercial plans, and which are in its own subsidised plans. The most recent release is May 2026, published in September. The [user guide](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/may-2026-omr-and-premises-in-bduk-plans-england-and-wales/uprn-level-release-user-guide-and-technical-note-for-premises-in-bduk-plans) explains the fields. The ones that matter:

- **current_gigabit**: whether your property already has a gigabit connection available.
- **future_gigabit**: whether a supplier has told BDUK it plans to build to you.
- **subsidy_control_status**: **White** (nothing gigabit now, and none likely within three years), **Grey/Black** (gigabit available or due within three years), **Under Review** (a supplier claims coverage or plans, but it isn't verified) or **Unassessed**.
- **bduk_gis_contract_scope**: **Initial** means you're in the confirmed scope of a Project Gigabit contract. **Deferred** means you're in the contract but outside the initial scope, and may only be added later.[^3]

If the files are too much, BDUK asks people with premises queries to email bduk.statistics@dsit.gov.uk and include their UPRN.

### Step 3: ask the builder

If you're in a contract, ask the named supplier for a date. Connecting Devon and Somerset also has a [postcode coverage search](https://www.connectingdevonandsomerset.co.uk/better-connection/coverage/), though it's a broader view than the BDUK data.[^4]

## Where Somerset's rollout stands

### South Somerset: Wessex Internet's contract

South Somerset, which includes Wincanton, is one of the two areas in the Project Gigabit contract for [Dorset and South Somerset](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/project-gigabit-network-build-contract-dorset-and-south-somerset), awarded to **Wessex Internet** in 2024. It started at around 21,400 premises. After several changes it was down to **18,510 premises** by July 2026, of which Wessex Internet had built to **5,030**, according to [BDUK data reported by ISPreview](https://www.ispreview.co.uk/index.php/2026/07/wessex-internet-tweak-dorset-and-somerset-uk-project-gigabit-broadband-contract.html).[^5]

That's real progress, but it also means nearly three in four contracted homes were still waiting at that point. The contract page gives no completion date, and the national Project Gigabit target is 2032.

### The rest of Somerset

[Connecting Devon and Somerset](https://www.connectingdevonandsomerset.co.uk/about/rollout/) says more than 159,000 premises across its region are due to be included in Project Gigabit, with a separate rollout bringing full fibre to more than 56,000 homes and businesses in earmarked areas. Parts of Exmoor are served by Airband's fixed wireless network instead.

## Why the voucher route has gone

For years, the fallback for homes left out of a big contract was a gigabit voucher: get your neighbours together and have a supplier build to you with government funding. That door is now shut in Somerset.

- BDUK [closed the voucher scheme to new projects in Devon and Somerset](https://www.ispreview.co.uk/index.php/2025/04/bduk-close-gigabit-broadband-vouchers-to-devon-and-somerset-uk.html) in April 2025, so it wouldn't overlap with the Project Gigabit contracts.
- Across the UK, suppliers could no longer [submit new voucher projects from 31 August 2026](https://www.ispreview.co.uk/index.php/2026/09/gigabit-broadband-voucher-scheme-closes-to-new-uk-projects.html). Existing projects can still be delivered.[^6]

So if your address is White, Deferred or outside every plan, there's no obvious scheme to jump the queue. That's the situation where waiting costs you the most.

## Using Starlink as a bridge

The case for Starlink while you wait rests on the terms. In October 2026, UK Starlink home plans were listed as [rolling monthly with a one-month minimum](https://broadbandswitch.uk/starlink-broadband-deals.html) and a 30-day trial, with Residential plans from £40 a month in some areas.[^7] If fibre arrives in a year, you cancel. The main sunk cost is the upfront kit fee.

Three things to weigh honestly:

1. **Prices move.** Starlink [raised most UK plans by £5 a month](https://www.ispreview.co.uk/index.php/2026/05/starlink-unexpectedly-hikes-uk-and-global-leo-satellite-broadband-prices.html) from June 2026. Budget for change, not just today's figure.
2. **Check the checkout terms.** Plans, upfront costs and any commitment-linked offers depend on your address and the date you order. Read the terms before paying.
3. **You need a clear view of the sky.** Most houses can fit a dish without planning permission, but listed buildings, conservation areas and roof heights have limits. My [planning permission guide]({{ '/starlink-dish-planning-permission-somerset/' | relative_url }}) covers the rules for Somerset.

### If you're under 10Mbps

There's another route worth knowing. Under the broadband [Universal Service Obligation](https://bt.com/about/bt/policy-and-regulation/keeping-the-uk-connected/a-universal-service-obligation), you can ask BT for a connection of at least 10Mbps if nothing qualifying is available and no publicly funded improvement is scheduled within 12 months. BT covers the work up to £3,400; above that, you can choose to pay the excess.[^8] It won't be gigabit, but it may be worth a request before you decide.

## Questions people ask

### Can I still get a gigabit voucher in Somerset?

Not for a new project. The scheme closed to new projects in Devon and Somerset in April 2025, and UK-wide from 31 August 2026.

### How do I find out if fibre is coming to my house?

Find your UPRN on FindMyAddress, then check it in BDUK's premises data. "Initial" contract scope is the confirmed build. "Deferred" may come later, or not.

### Is Starlink a contract I'll be stuck in when fibre arrives?

Home plans were rolling monthly in October 2026, so usually not. Check the terms at checkout, because offers change.

### My broadband is under 10Mbps. Is there anything else I can ask for?

A Universal Service Obligation request to BT, if nothing qualifying is available and no public scheme will improve it within 12 months.

### When will Project Gigabit finish?

The national target is 2032. Individual contracts don't promise a date for each house, so check your own address.
